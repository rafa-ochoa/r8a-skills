# Decision brief — on-demand reference

Consult for **new, costly, or substantially ambiguous product decisions**, not as a required document for every feature. Often a short conversation or task note is enough.

## Establish only what is needed

- **User / job:** who is trying to do what, and in what existing journey?
- **Problem / evidence:** what is observed, what is user-provided, and what remains a hypothesis?
- **Fit / alternatives:** does the existing experience already serve the goal? Compare the proposal with the strongest realistic alternative. Identify distinct user needs, benefits, and capabilities removed; apparent visual duplication may hide different jobs.
- **Smallest useful slice:** the minimum functional behavior and quality that genuinely benefits the user, within the requested platform or audience. Treat wider changes as separate proposals.
- **Product decisions:** which requirements are yours to investigate, and which must be decided by the user? Ask user-owned decisions in dependency order; don't repeat settled questions.
- **Success / risks:** observable acceptance criteria, edge cases, reversibility, and any privacy, permission, or data risks.
- **Deferred work:** optional improvements that don't compromise the promised experience.

## Decision rules

- If the request is already clear, **implement**; don't create a discovery gate.
- Resolve environmental facts through repository inspection rather than asking the user. If the expected application or integration is missing, clarify the intended project or authorized prototype scope before building.
- For one material unknown, ask one focused question with a recommendation and its downside.
- For multiple dependent unknowns, investigate and interview **automatically in rounds**, asking foundational choices before downstream details.
- Do not invent evidence, ask a generic questionnaire, or veto an authorized feature just because another design is possible. If an unverified assumption drives the recommendation, say what would change your mind; prefer a low-regret, reversible option when appropriate. Explain substantive concerns and offer a smaller test.
- Stop at sufficient clarity. Do not require an exhaustive tree, formal PRD, or repeated approval unless risk genuinely demands it.
